## Findings, ranked for small/medium shops

### 1. Language copies in Shopify sitemaps are registered as separate product URLs — not ticketed

- What happened: both multilingual Shopify shops list every product twice, under `/products/<p>` and `/en/products/<p>` (separate `sitemap_products_*` files under `/en/`). All 1,097 + 1,097 (Hauptstadt) and 6,303 + 6,300 (Debongout) were stored as product URLs. A crawl stores only one of them, because the hreflang canonical rule folds `/en/x` onto `/x` when it reads the page's head.
- Cause: `src/crawler/src/spider/discovery/sitemap/mod.rs:35` (`register`) registers every pattern-taken URL. Sitemap products are not fetched, so they carry no `PageIdentityEvidence`, and `canonical_product_url` (`src/crawler/src/spider/utils/url/canonical.rs`) can only apply its platform rule, not the language rule.
- Impact: doubles the scrape load for multilingual shops. The scraper's same-product rule treats a language segment as the same page (`src/crawler/src/scraper/same_product.rs:91`), so one spelling is retired as `DUPLICATE` on first scrape. Whichever is scraped first carries the identity, so a listing can end up captured in the shop's second language instead of its default.
- Fix direction: in `register`, drop a pattern-taken URL whose page without a leading language segment (`without_language_segment`, `canonical.rs:106`) is also listed and taken, so the unprefixed spelling wins. This is the sitemap's own evidence of the alternate. Optionally also read `xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang` from the sitemap.

### 2. The request cap leaves the same product sitemaps unread on every crawl — not ticketed

- What happened: Fireside splits 4,800+ products into 45 files of 200. The 25-request cap read 24 product files and left 20 unread, both times in the same order. Original Antique Furniture left 3 of 28 files unread. Reading took 13 s and 30 s, far below its time share (150 s of a 600 s crawl).
- Cause: `SitemapLimits::default` `max_requests: 25` (`src/crawler/src/spider/discovery/sitemap/reader.rs:44`) and the fixed product-first order (`reader.rs:259`, `products_first_files`). Nothing carries over between crawls, so files past the cap are never read; their products are found only by the crawl itself (Fireside: 285 outside the sitemaps).
- Impact: shops whose platform writes small sitemap files (Yoast with a low entries-per-file setting) get a stable, partial sitemap coverage.
- Fix direction: let the time share be the real bound and raise the request cap (60 would have read both shops), or rotate the start offset among an index's children per crawl so every file is read within a few crawls.
