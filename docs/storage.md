# Storage Design and Guardrails

This document describes **durable storage ownership, integrity and failure rules**, not an inventory of tables, columns, indexes, enum variants, SQL, Rust ports or deployed jobs. The current schema lives in [`migrations/`](../migrations/); code and tests define its concrete implementation. For event routes and worker custody see [event flow](events/flow.md) and the [durable-worker runbook](durable-worker-runbook.md). For bounded-context semantics see [ProductListing](product-listing.md) and the other owning domain documents. Examples of schema shapes below illustrate an invariant; they are not exhaustive schema specifications.

## General rules

- PostgreSQL owns business state and durable execution intent. Search documents and other read projections are rebuildable, not alternative authorities. Keep operational receipts and secrets out of CDC and projections unless a separately designed route requires them.
- Persist aggregate changes, their authoritative events, and any required local delivery intent atomically. Never hold a database transaction open across a third-party API call. Do not claim exactly-once external delivery: lease fencing, idempotency and recovery must account for a call succeeding when its local finalization does not.
- Constrain identities, foreign keys and valid state transitions in storage where practical. Decode and validate persisted values at adapter boundaries; corrupt or inconsistent rows are errors, not missing records or permission to reconstruct default state.
- Changes that can race must have an explicit lock order or optimistic-concurrency boundary. A conflict is not a not-found result; retries must preserve the original decision and idempotency semantics.
- Logical expiry is enforced when data is used, even if physical cleanup is delayed. Cleanup must be bounded and must not make expired credentials, proofs or receipts valid again.
- Schema changes and CDC selection require explicit migration and activation review. Do not infer a deployed route or maintenance job merely from a table, migration or CDK definition.

## Users and email-marketing consent

- The User's current account email and marketing consent are authoritative locally. Consent is purpose- and address-specific: changing an upstream identity or email never transfers permission. Anonymous email-only subscriptions do not create Users. Cognito's native signup request is not current consent; only verified, trusted signup confirmation or a completed double opt-in may grant it. Registration and proof replays must not restore a grant withdrawn later.
- A double-opt-in challenge is short-lived proof, not an audit log or a delivery guarantee. Persist only what is needed to verify its exact target, expiry and replay; never persist the raw token or rendered email. Check expiry at confirmation time, regardless of cleanup. Issue the proof before sending, do not wrap email I/O in its transaction, and treat definite rejection differently from ambiguous acceptance. Generic invalid-proof responses must not disclose whether a mailbox or challenge exists.
- Local consent decisions and their immutable-target synchronization intents commit together. Independent consent-decision ordering must fence stale User work, even when a concurrent profile update changes the User aggregate. Deduplicate by stable proof/action identity, not a request ID. An unchanged boolean does not imply an unchanged consent decision, and a database change alone does not imply provider delivery.
- Serialize decisions and worker work for the same recipient. Worker claims and completion must be fenced and recoverable after lost responses; grants must be rechecked against the latest verified decision and address ownership before external I/O. Expired grants cannot be applied later, while revokes must not simply expire into permission to send. Provider withdrawal must cancel obsolete local grants atomically and protect against an in-flight external grant; corrective revokes must not overwrite a newer verified decision or affect a reassigned address. Never convert an orphaned User grant into an anonymous grant.
- Keep provider-owned membership distinct from local consent. Do not echo provider-originated withdrawals as ordinary new outbound grants. For synchronization routing and repair custody see [marketing-consent event flow](events/flow.md#marketing-consent-sync-intent).
- Loops webhook receipts are bounded operational deduplication evidence, not consent history: retain only the authenticated body digest, event identity/time, necessary exact-mailbox/contact/list identifiers, disposition and expiry. The ordering/contact fence must outlive receipt cleanup and remain scoped to the exact mailbox. Apply a provider withdrawal, its User revision change, obsolete-grant cancellation, pending-proof invalidation and receipt in one transaction; a receipt cannot claim completion before those effects commit. A current local permission value does not replace provider suppression or send-time eligibility checks. See the [Loops preference-event contract](events/flow.md#loops-preference-webhooks).

## Notifications and watchlists

- PostgreSQL owns notification content snapshots and separate external-delivery intent. A notification's semantic cause supplies idempotency, not its public identifier. Retain notification history when a referenced listing is physically deleted; do not silently cascade through provenance. Delivery planning commits with notification creation; channel dispatch happens later under a fenced claim. A send/finalize crash can duplicate delivery, so the sender and recovery path must tolerate at-least-once attempts. See [notification event flow](events/flow.md#canonical-notification-delivery).
- Watchlist writes use optimistic concurrency. Maintain distinct active and notification-enabled intervals so notifications cannot be generated from events outside the applicable interval; compare against the immutable event time. Withdrawal is reversible and does not erase a retained watch or its quota occupancy; explicit physical deletion has different semantics. Tier reconciliation and watch changes must serialize around authoritative quota and listing lifecycle state.

## Parties, sources, auctions and ingestion

- Party, Partnership, membership and ListingSource access are authoritative in PostgreSQL. An active or dissolved Partnership and a retained source can block Party deletion; source access revocation removes only the requested grant. Do not leak contact details, credentials or provider configuration through safe administrative read models.
- Provider-specific ingestion configuration is persisted with its declared ListingSource method in one transaction. Missing, orphaned or invalid configuration fails closed. Authorize partner changes before mutation, preserve aggregate concurrency, and never expose webhook secrets in reads or logs.
- Auctions are source-scoped and can block source deletion. Commit their state changes with the corresponding immutable semantic events. Listing-owned auction and lot facts remain on the ProductListing aggregate; an optional Auction reference must point to the same source. Validate persisted schedules, identities and event shapes rather than silently repairing them.
## ProductListing events and revisions

- ProductListing is the authoritative write model. Distinguish aggregate write version, projection ordering and the source markers for derived content; enrichment does not impersonate a domain write. Its immutable domain/enrichment journal is the selected projection event source, not a generic outbox. Raw source capture, immutable raw revisions, normalization outcomes and provider-observation deduplication are separate concerns; raw evidence must not enter downstream canonical event consumers by accident.

## ProductListing ingestion command receipts

- Command-execution receipts deduplicate successful applications, not queue admission or business rejection. A repeated command with conflicting verified semantics fails closed; canonical/raw writes and the success receipt commit together. Retain receipts independently of listing/source deletion so deletion cannot make a previously executed command executable again. Keep receipts and raw provider evidence out of CDC, analytics and public history. See [ingestion submission](product-listing.md#asynchronous-ingestion-submission) and [CDC routing](events/flow.md#cdc-routing).

## Credentials and expiry

- Store only hashes or constrained short identifiers for long-lived access tokens and client secrets. One-time authorization/exchange credentials are consumed atomically; related credentials are revoked on owner deletion. Short-lived raw exchange material, where unavoidable, is restricted to that exchange and never enters logs, projections or CDC.
- Enforce expiration on authentication and redemption, not by assuming cleanup has run. A failed valid exchange rolls back atomically; cleanup must preserve any still-valid dependent exchange. Physical cleanup is operational maintenance, never a substitute for expiry checks. See [event flow](events/flow.md#aws-survivor-event-flow) for runtime recovery.

## Indexed reads and FX

- Indexes should serve actual identity, ordering and pagination contracts; verify them against query plans and migrations rather than mirroring every filter in this guide. Preserve deterministic tie-breaking for pagination and event history. Foreign-key restrictions and lock ordering must prevent creation of new references during deletion checks. Avoid indexing unbounded raw payloads or long URLs solely for convenience.
- PostgreSQL owns immutable FX snapshots and their complete, validated quote sets. Persisted snapshots, not the live provider, supply product read/write/search valuations. A sale observation pins its exact historical snapshot; ordinary product updates do not infer one. Current valuations choose an eligible snapshot at a fixed valuation time, while sold history uses its recorded observation. Missing or partial snapshots fail rather than guessing rates.
- OpenSearch pricing and saved-filter match facts are derived from authoritative source prices, pinned observations and snapshots. Search continuation must reuse the valuation basis selected on the first page; FX capture alone does not silently rewrite historic matches or trigger unrelated notifications. Periodic matching checkpoints advance only after completed eligible work, so retries and cancellations cannot skip a window. See [scheduled matching flow](events/flow.md#consumers-and-scheduled-matching).
