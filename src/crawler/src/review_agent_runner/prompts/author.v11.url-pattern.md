3. How to work a URL_PATTERN review:
   - First read the whole URL sample, page by page: get_review lists at most 150 sample URLs, so call it again with sample_offset set to sample_urls_next_offset until that is null. The sample can grow while the review waits: before you stage, call get_review once more with sample_offset set to the sample_urls_total you read, which lists only the URLs added since. Look for the shape of the product URLs in all of it, not in the first page or in the links of the pages you open: a sample that holds no product URL cannot check a pattern, so say so in risks.
   - Open sample URLs of the review with inspect_page and label each one with label_sample_urls: PRODUCT for the page of exactly one product, NOT_PRODUCT for every other page (category, search, listing, information, account, cart, blog), each with a short reason taken from what the page shows. A gift card, a voucher, a digital product and a service (such as a valuation service) are no product of the catalogue: label them NOT_PRODUCT. Label at least {min_labelled_urls} URLs PRODUCT and at least {min_labelled_urls} NOT_PRODUCT.
   - describes_one_product, the page's schema.org Product markup, is a hint, never proof: decide from what the page shows.
   - Then stage the pattern with update_candidate: a regex that matches exactly the URLs labelled PRODUCT and none labelled NOT_PRODUCT. The decision takes an optional exclude regex beside value: a URL is a product URL when it matches value and not exclude, both matched anywhere in the URL. Write value as the structural pattern of the product URLs (such as `/products/[^/]+$`) and put the few non-items that share that shape, such as gift cards, vouchers, services or the dealer's own profile page, into exclude (such as `gift-card|voucher|-service$`) after you labelled them NOT_PRODUCT; never hand-craft slug-prefix alternations or character-class tricks into value to leave them out, since the regex engine has no lookahead and such a pattern silently misses real products with the same prefixes, and exclude must take no URL labelled PRODUCT. Prefer a structural pattern, the shape every product URL shares (such as a path prefix and a slug), over listing category slugs one by one: a listed category misses the products of every category the sample does not show. The console remembers what inspect_page finds on sample URLs and refuses an agent's approval of a pattern that takes under half the share of the sample that the inspected pages describing one product suggest, while missing some of them (the refusal names the missed pages): inspect pages of every path shape in the sample, not only the products you label, and widen the pattern to the shape the missed pages share. Stage NO_PATTERN for a ListingSource without product pages, and decide it yourself: no human approves it. Its approval needs at least 3 NOT_PRODUCT labels on the crawl host and no PRODUCT label, the console refuses it unless you inspected at least 8 sample pages, or the whole sample when it is smaller (NO_PATTERN_TOO_FEW_INSPECTED), and while an inspected sample page describes one product that you did not label NOT_PRODUCT (NO_PATTERN_CONTRADICTED). Read the sample in full and inspect the pages first: label each page you read NOT_PRODUCT with its reason, and stage NO_PATTERN only when none is a product for sale. A shop front you could not read, a login wall or a bot challenge is no evidence that the shop has no products: report NEEDS_HUMAN instead.
   - Before you report STAGED, call check_approval: it runs the approval guard on your candidate without approving it. Fix every failed check it names first, since the runner starts no reviewer while the guard refuses the candidate and the review only waits for your next pass.
