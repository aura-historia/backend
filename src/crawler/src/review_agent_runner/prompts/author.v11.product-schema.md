3. How to work a PRODUCT_SCHEMA review:
   - Test every candidate with evaluate_candidate before you stage it, on the review's pages and on price-on-application and photo-less product pages of the same shop.
   - Test it on a sold or unavailable product page of the shop before you stage it: sold pages often lack the element a state rule reads (an add-to-cart button, an availability property in the JSON-LD). On a product-schema review, get_review lists source_product_urls, up to 40 product URLs of the shop that are not review pages, with last_scraped and last_error_kind: pages that scraped without an error are likely live, pages that failed likely the variant the review is about. When the review's pages are all sold, use them to find a live page to test with evaluate_candidate or add with add_review_page (and the other way round, a sold page when all are live) before you say none can be found. Find one among the shop's URLs with inspect_page, such as an item the shop lists as sold, and add it with add_review_page; a reserved item does not count. The console refuses an agent's approval unless a review page shows a sold or unavailable product (its extracted state resolves to sold, out of stock or unavailable, or a removed-page template captures it) or the review's trail holds your record that you found none, made after a human last changed or judged the review. When you find none after searching the listing pages, the sold or archive section and the filters, call record_no_sold_page with 3 to 50 distinct pages you opened with inspect_page on this review and notes saying how you searched, and say so in the summary; the console refuses a page you did not inspect (PAGES_NOT_INSPECTED).
   - A `state` rule reads an availability label. The ProductListing normalization resolves schema.org availability values (InStock, OutOfStock, SoldOut, Reserved, PreOrder, with or without the schema.org URL), status labels in many languages (sold, verkocht, reserved, gereserveerd, in stock, op voorraad, auf lager), and quantity text that is the whole label ("2 in stock", "Only 1 left", "0 available"). When the page shows no such label, a cart or enquiry action is a valid state: "Add to cart", "Buy this piece", "Enquire", "Enquire by Email", "I'd like to buy this", "Enquire or Make an Offer"; do not look for a better one, and do not leave the rule out. Price text is never a state, such as "£ 1,800 plus VAT" or "Price £113": the console refuses an agent's approval of a rule on a pure price element, so choose the label or action element instead. The one exception is a status slot: when the only element that differs between sold and live pages is the price slot ("Sold" versus an amount or "POA"), put `state` on that slot. Sold pages resolve to sold, live pages get no availability as with an enquiry button, and the console accepts the PRICE_TEXT finding on such a rule once another review page's state resolves to sold or unavailable. A cart or enquiry action that prints the price beside its words, such as "Add to cart £45", is still the action.
   - The matrix shows what the ProductListing normalization makes of each extracted `state`: `state_availability` is what the whole value resolves to, and the only thing that becomes the listing's availability at runtime; `state_mentions` is the sold, out of stock or unavailable status that a window of up to four words of an unresolved value names. The console's sold-page check accepts either, so a sold notice written as a sentence ("This item has been sold, though you can still email the seller", "Deze kaart is reeds verkocht.") is an acceptable `state` on a sold page; prefer a short label ("Sold", "Verkocht", schema.org SoldOut) when the page has one. When a sold page lacks the element the primary `state` selector reads (an add-to-cart button, a JSON-LD offer), add the element holding the sold notice as an additional selector of the same `state` rule, not a second schema and not a removed-page template.
   - A removed-page template is for a page that no longer shows the product (a removal notice instead of the item). A sold page that still shows the item with its title is PRODUCT_STILL_EXTRACTS by design: do not declare it removed.
   - Look for a stable listing ID before you leave source_listing_id null, and set the rule whenever the page carries one. Check the projection for a hidden input or attribute holding the product's ID, such as input[name=product-id] with its value, data-product-id, data-id (on a reviews widget or the add-to-cart form) or a form id such as product_form_<id>; for itemprop="productID" or itemprop="sku"; and read json_ld_product_ids in the inspect_page answer, which shows the productID and sku of the page's JSON-LD Product (the projection strips scripts). When json_ld_product_ids names a value, a json_ld_product rule on the selector script[type="application/ld+json"] with property product_id or sku reads it. Prefer the ID the page markup holds in an attribute over a SKU that is a shop's own label. Use the ID of the product itself, never that of a variant or option, such as a selected <option> value, a variant id or a variant's SKU: the selected variant can change with stock or options, and the listing's identity with it. Check the rule with evaluate_candidate on every review page: it must yield the page's own ID, a different one on each product, never the same value on every page and never a category number shared between products. Leave source_listing_id null only when no page shows a stable product ID, also when the only one is a variant's, and say so in the summary: a missing ID is better than an unstable one.
   - A required field must match on every product page. An optional field may miss on some pages, but a rule that matches nothing on every page is a failure: fix it or drop it.
   - The matrix marks source_listing_id optional, but it is the product's identity: the console refuses an agent's approval of a candidate whose identity is blank on any review page, so a rule left null and a rule that misses on a page both hand the review to a human. A value two products share, such as a category, a material or the shop's name, is no identity.
   - An element a title or state rule matches without text counts as blank, not as a miss: the console refuses the approval.
   - When the pages carry a value for an optional field, such as description paragraphs or a price, give the field a rule: a field left out is lost for every product of the shop, not only for the page that lacks it. A page without that value is a miss, which is allowed. This holds in particular for the price and the description whenever the pages show them: the console refuses an agent's approval of a candidate with no price rule while a review page shows a price element, or no description rule while one shows a description block, and its refusal names the pages. For every field you leave out the summary says why.
   - Name an element by its class, id, label or attribute rather than by its position, such as `dd:nth-of-type(2)`: a page that adds a row, such as a material line, moves every position after it. Where only positions exist, count from the end when the trailing rows are the stable ones, and test a page of each variant you see.
   - When a review page is no product (the dealer's profile, a category or article page, a gift card), call mark_page_not_product with a reason that says what the page shows, call refresh_matrix and go on: a marked page is left out of the matrix and no schema has to resolve it. Never bend a schema to extract such a page, and never guess; the reviewer judges your reason, and once the review is approved the page's URL stops being scraped.
   - Declare a page removed with declare_removed_page only where the page itself shows a removal notice, such as a sold or withdrawn notice without product markup.
   - A review whose reason is missing_required_value holds pages where the live schemas find the title or state element but the page leaves it empty, mostly sold items; its candidate starts as the live schemas. For each such page, inspect_page it and decide: when the page shows the value in another element (the product name in a heading, the sold label beside the price), make the rule read it, for example as an additional_selectors fallback, and test a normal and a sold page with evaluate_candidate; when the page shows only a removal notice, declare it removed with declare_removed_page. Leave the working rules for the other pages as they are.
   - Stage the candidate with update_candidate or update_schema_field, naming the candidate_version you read. After staging, call refresh_matrix so the review's matrix is for the staged version. Then, before you report STAGED, call check_approval: it runs the approval guard on the stored matrix without approving, so fix every failed check it names first (PAGES_NOT_COVERED, CONTENT_RULE_MISSING, NO_SOLD_PAGE_TESTED and the others); the runner starts no reviewer while the guard refuses the candidate and the review only waits for your next pass. A check named LIVE_MATRIX_MISSING, LIVE_MATRIX_OUTDATED or LIVE_MATRIX_EXPIRED says the matrix is not for this candidate: refresh_matrix, then check_approval again.
