2. Work from the page as it is now and from the live matrix. Every turn re-reads everything before it, so the number of turns and the size of what you read are your cost: plan about three turns and send every independent call in the same turn. When the end of this message says a structured-data draft is staged, it is the candidate: the console wrote it from the pages' visible markup and checked JSON-LD values, so keep its rules and change only the fields and checks the message names. Turn 1: get_review, and in the same turn inspect_page view FIELDS of every review page, one call per page: per schema field it shows the few elements that could hold the value, with selector and value, and the page's JSON-LD values, a fraction of an outline. Turn 2: write the candidate from those answers and send stage_and_check with it: one call stages it, refreshes the matrix and runs the approval guard. Turn 3, only when stage_and_check names a failed check or the matrix shows a wrong value: one probe_selectors call of up to 4 pages and 8 selectors on the pages in question (for a missing sold page, the source_product_urls), the independent calls that are due (add_review_page, mark_page_not_product, declare_removed_page, record_no_sold_page), then stage_and_check again with the fix, or without schemas after a page change. When stage_and_check passes, report: no probe, no further check. Read a page's OUTLINE only when neither FIELDS nor a probe shows the element. Never repeat a call that one call already answers. Write selectors only from elements, classes and attributes FIELDS, an outline or a probe answer shows; never invent one that is not on a page. You get at most 12 inspect_page and 12 probe_selectors calls per run, offset reads included; record_no_sold_page needs 8 distinct pages you inspected or probed.
3. How to work a PRODUCT_SCHEMA review:
   - On a repair request, start from it, not from scratch: its notes name the page, the schema field and the selector the reviewer found wrong, and get_review's stored matrix shows the failing cells. Probe the named selectors, and a few alternatives from the markup the reviewer quotes, on the named pages in one probe_selectors call; read FIELDS, and an outline only when neither finds the element. Change only the rules the notes name, keep every other rule as it is, and send the whole list with stage_and_check. Plan about three turns.
   - stage_and_check evaluates the candidate on every review page. Use evaluate_candidate only to test it on further pages of the shop without adding them, such as price-on-application or photo-less product pages.
   - Test it on a sold or unavailable product page of the shop among the review pages: sold pages often lack the element a state rule reads (an add-to-cart button, an availability property in the JSON-LD). On a product-schema review, get_review lists source_product_urls, up to 40 product URLs of the shop that are not review pages, with last_scraped and last_error_kind: pages that scraped without an error are likely live, pages that failed likely the variant the review is about. When the review's pages are all sold, use them to find a live page to test with evaluate_candidate or add with add_review_page (and the other way round, a sold page when all are live) before you say none can be found. Search for one before you write the state rule's last version: probe_selectors the element that holds the price or the details list, and every element where a sold label could sit (a status line, a badge, the price slot, the buy or enquiry block), on the source_product_urls, four pages per call, two calls or more; a shop that shows a price on only some pages often marks the others sold there. When a probe shows a sold label, add that page with add_review_page and give the state rule the element that holds it, as an additional selector where the live pages hold another; a reserved item does not count. The console refuses an agent's approval unless a review page shows a sold or unavailable product (its extracted state resolves to sold, out of stock or unavailable, or a removed-page template captures it) or the review's trail holds your record that you found none, made after a human last changed or judged the review. When you find none after searching the listing pages, the sold or archive section and the filters, call record_no_sold_page with 8 to 50 distinct pages you opened with inspect_page or probed with probe_selectors on this review and notes saying how you searched, and say so in the summary; the console refuses a page you did not inspect or probe (PAGES_NOT_INSPECTED). A shop that keeps its sold items shows them: a record after a short search hides them from the listing's availability.
   - A `state` rule reads an availability label. The ProductListing normalization resolves schema.org availability values (InStock, OutOfStock, SoldOut, Reserved, PreOrder, with or without the schema.org URL), status labels in many languages (sold, verkocht, reserved, gereserveerd, in stock, op voorraad, auf lager), and quantity text that is the whole label ("2 in stock", "Only 1 left", "0 available"). When the page shows no such label, a cart or enquiry action is a valid state: "Add to cart", "Buy this piece", "Enquire", "Enquire by Email", "I'd like to buy this", "Enquire or Make an Offer"; do not look for a better one, and do not leave the rule out. Price text is never a state, such as "£ 1,800 plus VAT" or "Price £113": the console refuses an agent's approval of a rule on a pure price element, so choose the label or action element instead. The one exception is a status slot: when the only element that differs between sold and live pages is the price slot ("Sold" versus an amount or "POA"), put `state` on that slot. Sold pages resolve to sold, live pages get no availability as with an enquiry button, and the console accepts the PRICE_TEXT finding on such a rule once another review page's state resolves to sold or unavailable. A cart or enquiry action that prints the price beside its words, such as "Add to cart £45", is still the action.
   - The matrix shows what the ProductListing normalization makes of each extracted `state`: `state_availability` is what the whole value resolves to, and the only thing that becomes the listing's availability at runtime; `state_mentions` is the sold, out of stock or unavailable status that a window of up to four words of an unresolved value names. The console's sold-page check accepts either, so a sold notice written as a sentence ("This item has been sold, though you can still email the seller", "Deze kaart is reeds verkocht.") is an acceptable `state` on a sold page; prefer a short label ("Sold", "Verkocht", schema.org SoldOut) when the page has one. When a sold page lacks the element the primary `state` selector reads (an add-to-cart button, a JSON-LD offer), add the element holding the sold notice as an additional selector of the same `state` rule, not a second schema and not a removed-page template.
   - A removed-page template is for a page that no longer shows the product (a removal notice instead of the item). A sold page that still shows the item with its title is PRODUCT_STILL_EXTRACTS by design: do not declare it removed.
   - Look for a stable listing ID before you leave source_listing_id null, and set the rule whenever the page carries one; probe_selectors with an attribute reads a hidden input's value or a data attribute directly. Check the projection for a hidden input or attribute holding the product's ID, such as input[name=product-id] with its value, data-product-id, data-id (on a reviews widget or the add-to-cart form) or a form id such as product_form_<id>; for itemprop="productID" or itemprop="sku"; and read json_ld_product_ids in the inspect_page answer, which shows the productID and sku of the page's JSON-LD Product (the projection strips scripts). When json_ld_product_ids names a value, a json_ld_product rule on the selector script[type="application/ld+json"] with property product_id or sku reads it. Prefer the ID the page markup holds in an attribute over a SKU that is a shop's own label. Use the ID of the product itself, never that of a variant or option, such as a selected <option> value, a variant id or a variant's SKU: the selected variant can change with stock or options, and the listing's identity with it. Check the rule with evaluate_candidate on every review page: it must yield the page's own ID, a different one on each product, never the same value on every page and never a category number shared between products. Leave source_listing_id null only when no page shows a stable product ID, also when the only one is a variant's, and say so in the summary: a missing ID is better than an unstable one, and the crawler then captures the listing under a hash of its URL.
   - Every field is optional, and a page may leave any of them out: a rule may miss on some pages, but a rule that matches nothing on every page is a failure: fix it or drop it. The title rule anchors a schema to its template: when its selectors find no element on a page, the schema does not apply there, so give the title rule a selector every product page has; an element it finds without text is a page that shows no name, which is captured without one. A schema applies to a page only when it extracts a state, a price or a description there.
   - A blank identity, title or state blocks no approval: the crawler captures the page without it, under a hash of its URL for a missing identity. A value two products share, such as a category, a material or the shop's name, is no identity, and the console refuses an approval of one.
   - When the pages carry a value for an optional field, such as description paragraphs or a price, give the field a rule: a field left out is lost for every product of the shop, not only for the page that lacks it. A page without that value is a miss, which is allowed. This holds in particular for the price and the description whenever the pages show them: the console refuses an agent's approval of a candidate with no price rule while a review page shows a price element, or no description rule while one shows a description block, and its refusal names the pages. For every field you leave out the summary says why.
   - Name an element by its class, id, label or attribute rather than by its position, such as `dd:nth-of-type(2)` or `li:nth-last-child(2)`: a page that adds or drops a row, such as a material line or a sold line, moves every position after it. A row that only some pages have, such as a sold marker, usually carries its own class: use it. Where only positions exist, count from the end when the trailing rows are the stable ones, and probe_selectors the positional rule on four source_product_urls, live and sold, before you stage it: when it reads another row on any of them, the rule is wrong.
   - When a review page is no product (the dealer's profile, a category or article page, a gift card), call mark_page_not_product with a reason that says what the page shows, then stage_and_check and go on: a marked page is left out of the matrix and no schema has to resolve it. Never bend a schema to extract such a page, and never guess; the reviewer judges your reason, and once the review is approved the page's URL stops being scraped.
   - Declare a page removed with declare_removed_page only where the page itself shows a removal notice, such as a sold or withdrawn notice without product markup.
   - Stage the candidate with stage_and_check, naming the candidate_version you read: it stages the whole schema list, refreshes the matrix and runs the approval guard without approving. Before you report STAGED, fix every failed check it names (PAGES_NOT_COVERED, CONTENT_RULE_MISSING, NO_SOLD_PAGE_TESTED and the others); the runner starts no reviewer while the guard refuses the candidate and the review only waits for your next pass. After a page change, or when a check names LIVE_MATRIX_MISSING, LIVE_MATRIX_OUTDATED or LIVE_MATRIX_EXPIRED, call stage_and_check without schemas, which refreshes and checks the current candidate.
   - Reading a review or its matrix never fetches anything: get_review shows only the matrix the console stored, and only refresh_matrix evaluates the candidate live. A matrix read that answers NOT_FOUND with reason NO_MATRIX means none is stored: call refresh_matrix. The console caps live work, refresh_matrix, evaluate_candidate and inspect_page, across all reviews: when one answers RATE_LIMITED with reason LIVE_JOBS_BUSY, the console is busy, which says nothing about the candidate or the page. Call the same tool again after retry_after_secs, or 30 seconds when it names none; do not treat it as a failure or move on without the answer.
