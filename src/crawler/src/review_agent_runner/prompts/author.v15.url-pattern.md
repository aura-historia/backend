2. How to work a URL_PATTERN review: induce one reusable product-detail regex for the shop from its URL sample alone: you read no page (the console refuses inspect_page on this artifact type) and you label no URL; use no external knowledge of the shop.
   - Read the sample with get_review: it lists the seed of the sample, its first 200 URLs in a fixed pseudorandom order, each as its path and ?query without scheme, host or fragment; you never see the rest of the sample, and the approval guard checks your pattern against all of it. Read further pages, with sample_offset set to sample_urls_next_offset, only while that is not null. The sample is unlabelled and may hold products, categories, brands, editorial, support and other pages.
   - Infer route structure before interpreting slug wording. A repeated identifier suffix, a dedicated product namespace or a distinguishing query parameter can be evidence. A generic flat slug-and-identifier shape is not enough by itself when non-product URLs of the sample share it: then require a product-only namespace, literal or query distinction, or leave that family out. Category-like words inside a product namespace do not by themselves make the route a category page.
   - Judge each route family on its own, then decide for the shop. One clear family does not justify a pattern when many other URLs plausibly look like product details but can be covered only by a generic shape non-products share: abstain rather than stage a pattern for a narrow subset. An isolated ambiguous extra family does not force abstention when the established families already account for the product-looking URLs. Include a family only on its own product-detail evidence; one URL can establish a rare family when its route structure supports it, but product-like wording, a lone numeric suffix or a generic namespace alone does not.
   - Preserve distinguishing literals, identifier positions, locale prefixes, suffixes, trailing slashes and query structure. Within an established family, generalise variable slugs and identifiers with delimiter-bounded segments, `[^/?#]+`. In a demonstrated product namespace, allow direct identifiers as well as optional intermediate slug segments when the sample shows both; do not require an intermediate slug because most URLs have one. Do not constrain identifier spelling, length, case or path depth unless that restriction separates the family from non-product routes of the sample; where depth varies, allow repeated intermediate segments instead of listing depths. Treat the query separately from the path. Combine the families by alternation, never by listing URLs, slugs or IDs, and invent no family the sample does not show.
   - Make every restriction earn its place, since a too narrow pattern is the commonest defect and a missed product is lost from the catalogue: a locale or country prefix (such as `/en-gb/` or `/fr/`) is optional, as in `(?:/[a-z]{2}(?:-[a-z]{2})?)?`, whenever URLs of the same family appear without it; inside a product namespace allow any depth, as in `(?:/[^/?#]+)*`, when depths vary; and never list categories in front of a product route, generalise that segment instead. Between a narrower and a wider structural form that takes no non-product URL of the sample, take the wider.
   - Before you stage, audit the pattern against the sample: for every URL that plausibly looks like a product detail and does not match, check whether a needless locale, fixed depth or identifier restriction excluded it, and whether leaving it out makes the pattern cover only a narrow subset. Check representative non-product-looking URLs for structural collisions. A gift card, a voucher, a digital product and a service (such as a valuation service) are no product of the catalogue: when they share the product shape, keep value structural and put them into exclude.
   - Stage the pattern with update_candidate as decision type PATTERN: value is one regex anchored with `^` and `$`, matched by full match against the URL's path and ?query exactly as the sample lists them (leave matching at its default); never put a scheme or host in it, and use no lookaround or backreferences. exclude is optional, a regex searched anywhere in the path and query: a URL is a product URL when value matches and exclude does not. Then call check_approval: it runs the approval guard without approving and refuses, among others, a pattern that matches no sample URL or every one. Fix every failed check it names, then report STAGED; the runner starts no reviewer while the guard refuses the candidate.
   - Abstain when the sample supports no defensible shop-wide structural distinction: report NEEDS_HUMAN, stage nothing, and say in the summary which route families you saw and why none separates the products. Never stage NO_PATTERN: a human decides it.
3. On a repair request, the reviewer names URLs the pattern misses or takes wrongly: check each against the sample, fix the family or the restriction it points at, audit the whole sample again and stage the result; abstain as above when the repair shows that no defensible distinction exists.
